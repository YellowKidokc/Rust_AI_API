## Ten Fights. One Equation. One Exit.

You broke something once. A cup, a lamp, your brother's toy. An adult
stood over the pieces and taught you the ritual: say you're sorry. So
you said it - and here is the detail you've never once stopped to
notice: you never believed the word would fix the cup. Not for a
second. No child in history has said sorry and then checked the floor
to see if the pieces climbed back together. You were four years old and
you already knew, in your hands, the two hardest facts in the universe:
what's broken doesn't unbreak - and saying the words doesn't make the
broken thing stop mattering. That's why, after you apologized, somebody
still had to get the broom.

Every fight on this page is an argument about who gets the broom.

## The equation

Real damage D. Restoring the victim costs T_A. The offender bears T_B.
In a closed system T_A = T_B. Define J = T_A/D and M = 1 - T_B/D.
Substitute: **J + M = 1.** Justice and mercy sum to one. Every point
given to one is taken from the other. There is no seat on this line
where the books close and nobody eats a bill they didn't run up.
Every political position is a seat. The fight is the seating chart.

## Immigration
*The fight you know:* "Secure the border / they broke the law" **vs** "No human is illegal / they are fleeing for their lives"

**border enforcement pole** rests on: *The law is the complete moral framework; breaking it is the decisive wrong.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Name one law you have broken that you think should carry the same consequence you propose for undocumented immigrants.**

**human dignity pole** rests on: *Moral obligation to the suffering person overrides all legal and economic constraints.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Name one group of people you would keep out at the border, and explain why your moral obligation does not apply to them.**

**market sorting pole** rests on: *Market efficiency is the primary moral and practical criterion.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Name one market outcome you would override with regulation, and explain why efficiency does not apply there.**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Citizens (higher taxes, service strain) and low-skilled natives (wage depression); Migrants (death, family separation) and low-skilled natives (wage depression); Migrants (no citizenship, exploitation risk) and low-skilled natives (wage depression)

**The sentence:** Every immigration plan makes someone who didn't break the glass sweep it up; the only fix is someone outside the room who volunteers to buy a new glass.

## Gun control
*The fight you know:* "Shall not be infringed / guns protect my family" **vs** "How many more children have to die"

**Right to bear arms / self-defense** rests on: *The right to bear arms is absolute and cannot be infringed.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Name one person you believe should not own a gun.**

**Gun control / public safety** rests on: *Removing guns will reduce gun deaths.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Should the police be disarmed?**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Law-abiding gun owners who lose the ability to defend themselves, and the victims of crimes that occur because police cannot respond in time.; The 40,000 annual gun death victims and their families.

**The sentence:** Both sides are right: guns save lives and guns kill lives, and no law can fix that trade-off.

## Healthcare
*The fight you know:* "Healthcare is a human right / Medicare for all" **vs** "Government medicine means rationing and you lose your doctor"

**universal coverage via single payer** rests on: *Healthcare is a human right, meaning society has an obligation to provide it regardless of cost.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **If a treatment costs $10 million and extends life by one month, does the human right require society to provide it?**

**market-based private insurance with employer mandate** rests on: *People should keep their existing insurance and doctor if they are satisfied.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **If you lose your job next month, do you keep your insurance and doctor?**

**free market / no government involvement** rests on: *No one should be forced to pay for another person's healthcare.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **If a man collapses on your doorstep with a heart attack and has no money, do you let him die?**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Taxpayers who did not cause the illness; The sick who cannot afford care (victims) AND those who pay inflated prices due to cost-shifting; The uninsured and underinsured (victims who are not fully restored) AND taxpayers who did not cause the illness

**The sentence:** Every healthcare plan forces someone who didn't cause the illness to pay; the fight is just over who sits on the bill.

## Abortion
*The fight you know:* "My body my choice" **vs** "Abortion is murder"

**bodily autonomy pole** rests on: *bodily autonomy is absolute and overrides any claim another entity has to use your body*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **If a fetus is a person, does that person have any rights that limit the pregnant person's bodily autonomy?**

**fetal personhood pole** rests on: *a fetus is a person from conception with full right to life*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Does a born person have the right to use another person's body without consent to stay alive?**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* the fetus eats the full cost (death); the pregnant person eats the full cost (forced bodily use)

**The sentence:** Both sides are right: one treats the fetus as disposable, the other treats the woman as an incubator - the line proves it.

## Criminal justice / incarceration
*The fight you know:* "Tough on crime / lock them up" **vs** "Mass incarceration is the new Jim Crow / rehabilitation not punishment"

**Punishment as deterrence and retribution (Tough on crime)** rests on: *Punishment is the primary mechanism for justice and deterrence.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **If punishment is the primary mechanism for justice, what do you do about crimes that are never reported or never solved?**

**Rehabilitation and decarceration (Mass incarceration is the new Jim Crow)** rests on: *Rehabilitation and decarceration reduce crime and are sufficient for justice.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **What do you do with an offender who completes rehabilitation programs and then commits the same crime again?**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Victims and communities eat the deficit: unrepaired harm (D - T_A = 0.95 * D) plus the cost of future crimes by unpunished offenders. The public also pays for rehabilitation programs.; Victims and their communities eat the deficit: they bear the unrepaired harm (D - T_A = 0.99 * D) while the offender bears only a fraction of the cost (T_B = 0.1 * D). The public also pays for incarceration through taxes.

**The sentence:** Both sides are right: the other side makes someone who didn't break it pay for it, because the math says someone always does.

## Welfare / Entitlements
*The fight you know:* "Social safety net / we take care of each other" **vs** "Dependency destroys people / welfare traps generations"

**Safety Net Pole** rests on: *The state must compel contributions because voluntary giving is insufficient to meet need.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **If a majority voted to cut your own benefits to fund a program you oppose, would you accept that as legitimate?**

**Dependency Concern Pole** rests on: *Work requirements and time limits prevent dependency and restore autonomy.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Would you accept the state mandating your own work schedule if it decided you were insufficiently autonomous?**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Taxpayers (including working poor who pay payroll taxes) bear the cost. They did not create the poverty of the recipients, but they pay for it.; The poor themselves bear the cost of unmet needs (homelessness, hunger, untreated illness). They did not create the systemic poverty, but they pay for it with their lives.

**The sentence:** Both sides are right: welfare helps the poor but hurts taxpayers; cutting it hurts the poor but helps taxpayers. The math never closes.

## Student loan forgiveness
*The fight you know:* "Cancel the debt / predatory system" **vs** "You took the loan, you pay it / why should the plumber pay for your degree"

**Debt cancellation pole** rests on: *The system is so predatory that borrowers bear no moral responsibility for the debt.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **If the system is entirely predatory, at what exact point did you, as a borrower, become aware of the predation and still choose to borrow?**

**Personal responsibility pole** rests on: *A contract is morally binding regardless of the circumstances under which it was signed.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Name one contract you believe should be voided due to unequal bargaining power (e.g., predatory lending, fine print).**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Borrowers who were misled or trapped by the system.; Taxpayers who did not borrow, did not attend college, or already repaid their own debts.

**The sentence:** Both sides are right: the borrower was wronged and the plumber should not pay—the real problem is no one volunteers to cover the trillion.

## Minimum wage
*The fight you know:* "Living wage / no one working full time should be poor" **vs** "Price floors kill jobs / the ladder's first rung gets cut off"

**Living wage pole** rests on: *A wage floor set above market equilibrium does not reduce employment because employers can absorb the cost from profits or pass it to prices without reducing labor demand.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Name one small business in your town that would survive a $5/hour wage increase without cutting hours or raising prices.**

**Price floor pole** rests on: *Any wage floor above market equilibrium necessarily reduces employment for marginal workers, and this job loss is worse than the poverty the floor aims to cure.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Name one industry where the market wage in 2023 was below $7.25/hour and workers in that industry were better off than if they had been paid $15/hour and lost their jobs.**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Low-wage workers and taxpayers eat the deficit via poverty wages and public assistance subsidies.; Marginal workers priced out of employment (the surplus labor) eat the deficit in lost jobs and income.

**The sentence:** Every minimum wage debate just picks who eats the gap between what labor is worth and what life costs.

## Climate policy: act now vs. crushing costs
*The fight you know:* "Existential crisis / act now whatever it costs" **vs** "Crushing energy costs on the working class for negligible temperature effect"

**Emergency action pole** rests on: *Any finite cost is justified to avoid infinite damage.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **What is the maximum cost in jobs and energy poverty you would impose on a single working family today to avoid a 0.1°C temperature rise in 2100?**

**Cost constraint pole** rests on: *If the benefit is negligible, the cost is unjustified.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **At what time horizon does the benefit of action become non-negligible, and why is that horizon not the relevant one?**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Future generations eat the full damage D. No restoration. No cost on emitters.; Today's working class eats the cost (T_B) while future generations receive partial restoration (T_A < D, because D is intergenerational and cumulative). The deficit is the remaining damage not averted.

**The sentence:** Both sides are right: one leaves the bill to the unborn, the other to the working class; the line proves no one on earth can close it.

## Affirmative action
*The fight you know:* "Repair historic injustice / level the playing field" **vs** "Fighting discrimination with discrimination / judge by merit"

**Repair pole: transfer resources to historically disadvantaged group to compensate for past discrimination.** rests on: *Repairing historic injustice requires transferring cost to those who did not create the damage, because the original perpetrators are mostly dead or unidentifiable.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **Would you accept a policy that makes your child bear a cost for damage your great-grandparent caused, if your family immigrated after the damage was done?**

**Merit pole: judge individuals by qualifications, not group identity; no race-based preferences.** rests on: *Merit as measured by test scores and grades is a fair and neutral basis for selection, and race-conscious repair is unjust discrimination.*

> Ask its best advocate one question: **If two students have the same test score, but one had access to test prep and the other did not, is the test score a fair measure of merit?**

*Who eats the deficit, pole by pole:* Current non-disadvantaged applicants (white, Asian) who are rejected or have lower chances due to race-conscious policy. They bear the cost of repair for damage they did not create.; Historically disadvantaged groups continue to bear the damage of lower opportunity and representation. No transfer occurs.

**The sentence:** Both sides are right: your plan makes innocent people pay for damage they didn't cause—and so does yours.

## The chorus

Ten fights. Twenty-three poles. Read the cards again and watch the
shape repeat: every accusation each side has thrown for decades is
TRUE - your plan makes someone who did not create the damage eat the
cost - and every premise, asked one honest question, dies in its own
advocate's mouth. That is not ten coincidences. That is one line,
J + M = 1, wearing ten costumes. You were handed a seat on it before
you were old enough to ask what it was a seat ON.

## The point off the line

The algebra permits exactly one exit: a cost-bearer from OUTSIDE the
ledger - neither victim nor offender nor conscripted bystander - who
volunteers, absorbs the full cost, has the capacity to survive the
absorption, and never denies the damage was real. Human forgiveness
and human generosity are noble, and they only move the deficit along
the line: the volunteer relocates the cost onto willing shoulders.
Retiring it requires the external configuration. History offers one
candidate whose founding claim is exactly that configuration - and
whose movement produced the only non-collapse reversal in 2,500 years
of institutional data. The door opens from your side only.
